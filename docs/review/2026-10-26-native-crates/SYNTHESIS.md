# Native-crate review: synthesis

Date 2026-10-26. Lead #2 (Fable), from thirteen crate-level reports in this directory, each written by an Opus reader or synthesizer who read every source file (or every shard report) of its crate. This document reads only those reports; every `path:line` below is carried from them and names the report it came from. Shard reports (`*--A-*.md` and siblings) hold the full evidence.

Stance, per ember (2026-10-26, via the coordinator): **rescue and repair as much functionality as possible instead of just killing it.** Section C is a triage, not a kill list. "Delete the twin" (ATLAS law 13) means pick the better twin, repair it, delete the other, never both. Every surface is ON for the demo: ssh shell, web docuverse, SPK grains, Hermes residents, PAY, Discord, fleet, fn, the SDKs.

## Executive summary

1. **The Rust is the size of the whole founding budget.** `native/` plus `prover/` hold about 290K lines of Rust (resource-client 113.8K, grain-runtime 55.9K, spk-host 54.7K, private-backend 26.1K, prover 8.0K, the rest ~31K) beside 24K lines of `deploy/`. ATLAS §7 budgeted "rust/ — interpreter + node + FFI marshalling ONLY" inside a ~290K total. The Rust alone has eaten it.
2. **The wire is right; the economy is not.** Every reader found the same sound core: Lean authors intents and admits them; Rust signs Host-printed bytes after a local Lean consent check, sends exact retained bytes, never resends in transport, and asks the Host to decode replies. No fail-open authority path was found in any crate. But Rust (plus operator JSON) authors the *content of every law* the shell installs, every non-provider *price*, the *routing* that is the sole gate on 69 operator-only ops, the *retry policy* (four contradictory ones), the provider-key *grant and quota* domain (mini-keys), the SPK *quota*, the sealed-market *allocation*, room *membership-for-payment*, and private-room *key entitlement*. Section B ranks 30 such sites with the Lean declaration that should own each.
3. **One missing Lean concept explains most of the lost-reply holes.** Three crates approximate "this exact retained call can never be admitted" by local inference (process liveness, ledger heights, header shapes, string matching). grain-runtime wedges a purse forever on one upstream timeout or one 429; spk-host fences a whole grain for 19–51 minutes on one `contention`; resource-client reads every 255 frame as "refused, retry is safe" at four sites while Lean also answers 255 for `unavailable` and for any thrown handler. The fix is a Host-returned **never-admissible witness** plus a **machine-readable no-effect class** on `Outcome` (resource-client Q1–Q9, grain-runtime Q2–Q6, spk-host Q1). Everything else in the retry story is Rust refactoring toward one custody machine that already exists (`mini_sdk::custody`).
4. **The surfaces ember wants on have mostly never run in the shape that ships.** The ACP Hermes controller has never run with `--network none` and its own bridge; neither shipped Hermes unit can open a scoped controller (install-controller renders one of three required variables and writes no registration); the room resident has no recorded run; the SPK broker, resident privilege and recovery code have no recorded run; the mini-keys broker has no end-to-end run; the Discord mirror is not packaged; pay-to-enrol lands on a proxy-mode row that cannot sign; `mini-sdk`'s `flow::Client` has no caller; `mini-sdk-ts` is imported by nothing. These are the rescues (section C), and most are S or M.
5. **Three crates cannot build in the tree today** (since the root workspace landed in `295cab11`, 2026-10-08): `prover/`, `native/hyperdocument-link-store`, and `native/bend-ir2-proof` (also needs a breadstuffs symlink). Their tests and benchmarks have silently stopped running; `check-prover-glue.sh` still passes because it only compares bytes (ATLAS law 8).
6. **Twins are the mass.** ~15 hand-rolled custody machines beside `mini_sdk::custody`; three traffic-privacy stacks (8.9K, none deployed); three dispatch generations in grain-runtime (~5.5K, ~2K duplicated); nine lifecycle skeletons in spk-host; `credentials.rs` compiled into three crates by `#[path]`; durable byte-CAS written four times; memfd sealing three times; four Rust copies of Lean's HTTP header allowlist; three disagreeing consent op lists; 159 literal op numbers bypassing the generated registry constants; ~80 custody `type` strings with no Lean declaration.
7. **The shared-uid hosted shell is the single largest live risk** and it is already a sibling-lane decision (split tenancy, phase 3.2). Every member can read every other member's seeds and the sponsor's custody copies; one member can hold the single Host thread for 600 s.
8. The finite prefix (section F) is three tiers: **demo** (repairs that make the week-1 surfaces actually run: ~25 S/M items, two Lean items on the critical path), **coherence** (one custody machine, one wire for op numbers and consent, Lean-emitted laws and tables, the never-admissible witness), **nice** (merges and research rescues). Deletions are the last step of each merge, about 35K lines in total once the kept twins are repaired, against ~290K.

**Verdicts, one paragraph each.**

- **resource-client.** Sound wire, sprawling product. The deployed demo (shell, documents, boards) is ~41K of 114K; chat, pay, fleet and private rooms raise that to ~58K; the rest is research (traffic stacks 8.9K), unreached (fn consumer 3.6K, selected exchange 2K, session re-enroll 1.2K) or dead. Its two systemic defects are the four retry philosophies and the 255-as-refusal reading. Its laws are Rust-authored. It is the crate where "delete the twin" buys the most, because the better twin (`mini_sdk::custody`, `replan.rs`, the generated registry) already exists in-tree.
- **grain-runtime.** A careful custody envelope wrapped around an economy Rust invented. It never speaks the Host wire; every effect is a `mini` subprocess on one blocking thread. Three dispatch generations, three one-incident receivers and an unrun 2K-line config migration sit on the hot path. Nothing in it runs on the public node; the production topology (`--network none` + bridge) has never run; neither shipped unit can open a scoped controller. The demo-minimal controller is ~24.5K (~31.7K with the room resident, which must first be made to run).
- **spk-host.** The authority boundary is mostly right (every request admitted by Lean, every lost reply a lookup). Its demo risks are availability and custody: a generation-wide dispatch fence, a resident that runs as the operator with no filesystem hardening, an unmeasurable sandbox floor, 35–47 s per GET, and a package identity decided by a breadstuffs-era git dependency. Four header-allowlist copies and nine lifecycle skeletons. ~4.9K deletable now, ~2K by merging; the demo build is ~33–38K.
- **private-backend.** Serious research (ePrint 2024/677 agreement, ACSS-Id, Beaver evaluation) with real crash tests, but no surface, no transport, no Host op, not in the workspace, and a privacy-critical rule (one-use row spending) that is a Rust copy of a proved Lean `reserve` that is never called. Zero lines for the demo; rescue as an honest in-process artifact and as key-epoch custody substrate.
- **prover.** An island and unbuildable. Three Lean-emitted dispatch adapters follow the sanctioned pattern; everything else (verifier verdicts, sumcheck wire, field constants) is hand-authored and regrew after the 2026-08-09 deletions. Fix the workspace exclusion so its conformance tests run again; do not wire it for the demo.
- **mini-sdk / mini-sdk-ts.** Half of mini-sdk is the best-tested utility code in the tree and is used everywhere; the other half is the one signing path the CLI *should* reuse and has drifted from Lean in four ways. Highest value per effort rescue in the review.
- **inference-scheduler, pay-watcher, link stores.** The sqlite store is the most load-bearing small crate and decides almost nothing; fix its exit-4 conflation and ship it. pay-watcher decides too much of "what counts as a payment" and can lose enrollments for good through an operator env floor. inference-scheduler is a whole policy in Rust; it serves only the homelab row. The old link store is unbuildable and goes once its fixture moves.
- **discord-entrance, spk-rpc.** The entrance's dedup is good; the roster is a Rust identity binding Lean never sees; the mirror wedges on any non-2xx and is not packaged. spk-rpc is vendored Sandstorm schemas plus a thin adapter; 17 of 28 schemas are compiled by nothing.
- **verifier, mini-keys, test-provider.** The verifier is correct and tiny; `verify-mldsa` cites a Lean declaration that does not exist. mini-keys is a second authority domain in Rust whose grant caps are checked against the controller's claims rather than the request. The test provider is a fixture that ships in every candidate.
- **tiny crates and shared root.** Two shipped crates (custody rule, API-path grammar) are fine; one Rust-only policy (compatible-upgrade allowlist) and one hand twin (`checked_prefix` vs `validApiPrefix`). The `#[path]` shared-source pattern should become a crate. `hybrid_kem.rs` claims there is no pure-X25519 path; `channel.rs` is one.
- **deploy/.** The packager is the only commit→pinned-binary path and is careful. The tree also carries dead chains (GitWeb ingest root path, `--capsule`), probes that no longer compile, 7.3K lines of never-shipped MUD data, 25 documented doc contradictions, and units whose binary paths disagree five ways. It decides no Lean-owned semantics.

## E. The scholarship: what this is, against the literature, and what it should become

Each principle lands on a crate or seam. Citations verified against the PL corpus (`~/Downloads/papers.jsonl.gz`) are marked †; the systems papers are cited from their standard venues.

### E.1 Object capabilities: a reference is not ownership, so stop making the cookie a key

Dennis and Van Horn, *Programming Semantics for Multiprogrammed Computations*, CACM 9(3), 1966, defined a capability as an unforgeable reference that *is* the authority; Miller, *Robust Composition* (PhD, Johns Hopkins, 2006) made the discipline compositional: no ambient authority, authority only by designation, revocation by interposition (caretakers). Shapiro, Smith and Farber, *EROS: a fast capability system*, SOSP 1999, showed it is cheap. Miller, Van Cutsem and Tulloh, *Distributed Electronic Rights in JavaScript*, ESOP 2013†, is CapTP; Spritely Goblins (Lemmer-Webber, 2020–) and OCapN carry it today.

Minidregg's README states the thesis correctly: "a reference is not ownership". The Rust breaks it in exactly the places the readers ranked highest:

- **spk-host custodial signing** (B #8): the static cookie *is* a signing key, permanent, unrotatable, and with Caddy injection it is one key for every stranger. Capability discipline says: the cookie should designate a **revocable caretaker** (a lease resource admitted by Lean, with `notAfter` and a revocation path), and the signature should name the caretaker, not impersonate the participant. The sibling's R3 (session leases) and R2 (one `__Host-` cookie module) are this; the Lean counterpart is a declaration of custodial delegation that `Kernel/ApplicationDispatchReceiver.lean` can check.
- **mini-keys** (B #9): a bearer ticket bound to a body digest is a capability; checking caps against the controller's *claims* is ambient authority by the back door. Bind the ticket to a Lean provider-reserve receipt that already carries `(model, max_tokens, height)`.
- **Discord roster** (B #17): a root-owned JSON file granting room authority is ambient. The door's Lean-admitted session is the designation; the bridge speaks under a Lean grant, not its own key.
- **Shared-uid hosted shell**: the uid is ambient authority over every member's seeds. Split tenancy (sibling 3.2) is the minimum; Melicher, Shi, Potanin and Aldrich, *A Capability-Based Module System for Authority Control*, ECOOP 2017†, is the in-language version: `mini shell`'s verb grammar is today the only fence, and a module that cannot name a path cannot read it.

### E.2 Mechanism versus policy: quotas, prices and routing are policy

Levin, Cohen, Corwin, Pollack and Wulf, *Policy/Mechanism Separation in Hydra*, SOSP 1975: the kernel provides mechanisms; policies are parameters supplied by a higher authority. ATLAS §7 is the same sentence ("rust/ … authors nothing"). The review's B-list is, almost row by row, policy that landed in the mechanism layer: the grain tariff (B #2), SPK quotas (B #14), the inference scheduler (B #16), restart rates, provider-key grants (B #9), body bounds and host budgets (B #29). Hydra's answer is not "delete the mechanism" but "make the policy a parameter the authority supplies": for minidregg that is a **Lean-emitted policy table** per grain/world (tariff, quota class, restart budget, bounds) that Rust *loads* and Lean *admits*, replacing operator JSON that Lean never sees. The job law already works this way (`Kernel/Job.lean` → `gen-joblaw.py`); B #6 says make every law and table go that way.

### E.3 Saltzer and Schroeder: fail-safe defaults and least privilege

Saltzer and Schroeder, *The Protection of Information in Computer Systems*, Proc. IEEE 63(9), 1975. Violations found: **permit-all default laws** on every fleet-joined account, member account, Hermes till and private room cell (B #6; `{"type":"all","predicates":[]}`); the **fail-open audit** in mini-keys (B-F7, writes continue when the audit cannot be written); **silent refusals** in spk-host (unauth → silent close; helper stderr to `/dev/null`; 255 frames not retained), which also breach ATLAS law 10 ("refusal must be loud"); the resident **running as the operator** with no filesystem hardening (least privilege); the **pay-watcher unit** writable over all of `/var/lib/mini`; the **operator `cp` as root** into an app-owned directory. Each has an S fix named in C. The principle also settles the default for B #27: a key that never expires is not a fail-safe default.

### E.4 Lost replies, idempotency, "exactly-once is a lie"

Lamport, *Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System*, CACM 21(7), 1978, gives the model; Garcia-Molina and Salem, *Sagas*, SIGMOD 1987, the compensation shape; Helland, *Idempotence Is Not a Medical Condition*, CACM 55(5), 2012, the practical rule: every message must be safe to receive twice, and the receiver, not the sender, owns deduplication. Ramalingam and Vaswani, *Fault tolerance via idempotence*, POPL 2013†, formalise exactly minidregg's aim: a monad whose operations are idempotent by construction given a durable identity, so retry is always safe.

The Lean side already owns the right primitive: replay by exact call bytes (resource-client §5.1 matrix, sound), `soltx:` nullifiers in PAY, `FleetTurnReceiver.receiveLoaded`'s lookup-before-admit. What is missing is **the receiver telling the sender which outcomes had no effect**. Three recommendations, each on a named seam:

1. **A no-effect class on `NativeHostCodec.Outcome`** (resource-client Q4; grain-runtime Q2–Q5; spk-host Q1): Lean answers every proven no-append outcome positively (the `NO_RECORD` frame spk-host already keys on), so Rust never infers "safe to retry" from a 255, a phase string, a `repr` path or a header shape.
2. **A never-admissible witness** for a retained exact call (an expired deadline or consumed nonce the Host attests) replaces the three "absent + submitter stopped" implementations and the two expiry arithmetics (B #3). Until it exists, "absent" must remain *uncertain, keep the hold, block the tool* — liveness lost, never a double effect.
3. **One custody machine** (`mini_sdk::custody`, parameterised) with Helland's rule built in: a durable identity before the frame leaves; a rerun without its record is refused (resource-client §5.4 point 5; today `credit pay`, `well mint`, `activity submit` mint fresh nonces). Sagas' compensation appears as **settle-by-anchor** (grain-runtime R8): a settle that names the reserve receipt is a compensating action Lean can check; an auto-settle that infers the reservation is not.

### E.5 Sandboxing: the grain model is right, the floor is unmeasured

Sandstorm's grain model (Varda, 2014–; one app instance per grain, per-grain uid, supervisor-mediated capabilities over Cap'n Proto) is what spk-host implements, and spk-rpc vendors its schemas unmodified. Watson, Anderson, Laurie and Kennaway, *Capsicum: practical capabilities for UNIX*, USENIX Security 2010, and CloudABI (Schouten, 2015) are the "no ambient OS authority" versions; bubblewrap and seccomp-bpf are the Linux mechanisms in use. Two findings: the **resident** (supervisor) runs as the operator on the host mount namespace with a filter that denies only namespace calls, which is the opposite of Capsicum's model where the supervisor is the least-privileged party that parses untrusted bytes; and the **floor cannot be measured** because the audit runs against a superseded spawn path. Recommendation: (a) render the resident's hardening drop-in now (S), (b) per-grain resident uid when K-SPK splits keys (L), (c) make the sandbox audit a gate that red-proofs itself (ATLAS law 8) before any stranger reaches EtherCalc, (d) close the seccomp gaps against Sandstorm's own list. For Hermes workers, the bwrap launcher has **no seccomp at all** while the SPK path has one: one policy, two consumers.

### E.6 Verified core, untrusted shell: the TCB must be exhibited, not assumed

Klein et al., *seL4: formal verification of an OS kernel*, SOSP 2009; Leroy, *Formal verification of a realistic compiler*, CACM 52(7), 2009; Protzenko et al., *EverCrypt*, IEEE S&P 2020, and Ahman et al., *Project Everest: Perspectives from Developing Industrial-Grade High-Assurance Software*, TOPLAS 2026†: the proof boundary is drawn explicitly, the unverified residue is enumerated, and verified code is *extracted* into the shell rather than re-written there. O'Connor et al., *Cogent: uniqueness types and certifying compilation*, JFP 2021†, is the same pattern for systems code.

Minidregg's `Compiler/CredentialSignatureIO.lean` does this honestly for the verifier ("does not prove the crypto implementation") and `Kernel/PayEnrolReceiver.lean:1209-1219` states the SSHSIG soundness as an uninhabited parameter. Three places do the opposite: the **prover** regrew hand-written verifier verdicts beside Lean-emitted adapters (B #30); **objective-emit**'s `runtime.c` transcribes `stepRaw` with no refinement theorem; **signed-api-path** and the store-codec/hiding twins restate Lean predicates in Rust. The Everest pattern says: extract. Lean `@[export]` of the small decidable predicates (`validApiPrefix`, `requestSafeFor`, the verb-tag table, the consent list, `Kernel.Bounds`) deletes the twins without a line of new Rust; and the in-process Lean codec the sibling's R5 plans (a Lean static library) removes the 4–5 Host processes per SPK request that make a GET take 35–47 s. ATLAS law 6 ("wire-first; a capability without its wire is orphaned") is the CI side of this: `prover` and the two link stores stopped building and nothing went red.

### E.7 Typed wire and derive-the-codec: one wire, generated

Delaware, Suriyakarn, Pit-Claudel and Chlipala, *Narcissus: correct-by-construction derivation of decoders and encoders from binary formats*, ICFP 2019†, and EverParse (Ramananandro et al., USENIX Security 2019) derive encoder/decoder pairs with round-trip proofs from one format specification. Cap'n Proto (Varda, 2013) is the typed-schema wire spk-rpc already vendors. Saltzer, Reed and Clark, *End-to-End Arguments in System Design*, TOCS 2(4), 1984, and Clark, *The Design Philosophy of the DARPA Internet Protocols*, SIGCOMM 1988, are the narrow-waist argument: one simple, universal wire with the intelligence at the ends.

Minidregg has the waist (`u32 LE len ‖ op ‖ payload`, one allowlist that matches the registry byte for byte) and then widens above it: 159 literal op numbers, three consent lists, an unregistered 220–233 namespace, ~80 custody `type` strings with no Lean declaration, hand JSON layouts (`room_schema.rs`, `"1008"`/`"1009"`), two agent wires that disagree, and `thin-proxy.py` locating a slot list with `difflib`. Recommendation: the registry (`protocol/host-operations.json` → `host-operations.rs`) is already a generated codec seam; extend it to **emit** the consent list, the response markers, the per-op bounds, the room layout and the agent wire types, and make the registry check scan for literals. ATLAS §7 already names "derived canonical codec with a generic round-trip theorem" for `Compiler/`; this is the same move applied to the host wire.

### E.8 Session types and choreographies for the host/client protocol

Honda, Yoshida and Carbone, *Multiparty asynchronous session types*, POPL 2008†; Carbone and Montesi, *Deadlock-freedom-by-design: multiparty asynchronous global programming*, POPL 2013†; Giallorenzo, Montesi and Peressotti, *Choral: Object-oriented Choreographic Programming*, TOPLAS 2024†; the Scribble protocol language (Yoshida, Hu, Neykova, Ng, TGC 2013). The lesson is not "adopt a session-type checker" but that a protocol with phases (reserve → send-boundary → outcome → settle; create → begin → claim → completion → stop) should have **one global description from which each party's local state machine is projected**. grain-runtime has three dispatch generations, spk-host nine lifecycle skeletons, resource-client fifteen custody machines: each is a hand-written local projection of a protocol nobody wrote down globally. Recommendation: write the two choreographies (the grain purse lifecycle; the SPK lifecycle/dispatch) as Lean data, prove the projection well-formed, and generate the Rust state machines' *skeletons* from them — the same emit-never-imitate law applied to control flow. The payoff is that the Lean side then knows exactly which outcome closes which phase, which is what the never-admissible witness and the no-effect class need.

### E.9 Algebraic effects and handlers: "typed effects that computation proposes"

Plotkin and Pretnar, *Handlers of Algebraic Effects*, ESOP 2009†; Leijen, *Type directed compilation of row-typed algebraic effects*, POPL 2017† (Koka); Lindley, McBride and McLaughlin, *Do be do be do*, POPL 2017† (Frank); Wu, Schrijvers and Hinze, *Effect handlers in scope*, Haskell 2014†; Brachthäuser, Schuster and Ostermann, *Effects as capabilities: effect handlers and lightweight effect polymorphism*, OOPSLA 2020†. The last is the exact shape of minidregg's thesis: an effect is a *capability to request*, the handler is the authority that *admits*, and the type says which effects a computation may propose. ATLAS §7's `Effects/` ("each effect ONE declaration … from it, DERIVED") is an effect-handler registry.

Where the Rust violates it: grain-runtime's MCP tool table and its 380-line foreground preflight (R22) are a hand-written effect signature; the resident's tool restriction (`restrictTools:false`) lets generic writes bypass the paid turn (R6) because the effect row and the payment are two effects, not one; `hermes_handoff` decides which resident a summon selects. Recommendation: the tool catalogue a grain may propose is a Lean-emitted effect signature (the same artifact that produces the MCP schema), and a paid room write is **one effect** (payment ⊗ write) with one handler, so there is nothing to bypass. The handler's result type is the three-state outcome of E.4.

### E.10 Append-only authenticated stores and receipts

Merkle, *A Digital Signature Based on a Conventional Encryption Function*, CRYPTO 1987; Laurie, Langley and Kasper, *Certificate Transparency*, RFC 6962, 2013 (consistency and inclusion proofs over an append-only log); Irmin (Gazagnaire et al., OCaml 2014) and Noms (2016) as branch-consistent content-addressed stores. Minidregg's `receipt_continuity` already walks consistency; what it lacks is **inclusion**: a confirmation is anchored to a point `(acceptedCount, worldRoot)`, not to the call's transaction (B #24, E S10), so a lying or rolled-back Host can confirm a call it never admitted and the client stops retrying. CT's answer is an inclusion proof per receipt (resource-client Q16: an opening from op 151). The sqlite store's `head-anchor` sidecar is a rollback detector in the same family and should be documented as part of the backup contract. For the helm target (CLAUDE.md north star: "review verdicts and land-receipts as receipts on chain"), inclusion proofs are what make a receipt portable beyond the box that issued it.

### E.11 What it should become (the one-paragraph version)

A **Lean-authored protocol** (choreographies for the two lifecycles; effect signatures for every tool; a registry that emits ops, markers, bounds, consent lists and layouts) **projected into one Rust custody machine** (`mini_sdk::custody` + `flow::Client`) that classifies only by Lean's typed outcome, retries only by exact bytes or by a Lean-declared retry-safe class, and never infers non-commitment locally; **capabilities as leases** (cookie → revocable caretaker; ticket → Lean reserve receipt; roster → Lean session) instead of keys and files; **policy as Lean-emitted tables** the mechanisms load; **the TCB exhibited** (verifier, bwrap floor, Caddy) and the small decidable predicates exported rather than mirrored; and **receipts with inclusion** so they travel. Every item in that paragraph is a row in C with a size.
