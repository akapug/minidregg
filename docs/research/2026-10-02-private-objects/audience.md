## 2. The proposed contract, before the crypto suite

**PROPOSED state:** `(object identity, admitted parent transition, authority generation, audience commitment, encryption epoch, recipient-device generation commitment, history policy, mode, pending transition ID)`. Authority generation, encryption epoch and delivery-domain population remain separate. An authorized device key change is not automatically a membership change; a transport participant is not automatically an object reader.

Modes are `Active(e)`, `Frozen(e,T)`, `RekeyPrepared(e+1,T)` and `Active(e+1)`. Prepared ciphertext may be retained privately or in a staged durable object, but staging grants no right to decrypt or invoke. The transition ID and exact command bytes must survive uncertain replies; reconciliation asks whether that operation already committed before constructing another.

The reader contract should say:

* A current member can request current observations only under Mini's current observation rules, and decrypt only the epochs/intervals delegated to it. Key possession does not itself authorize a Host query.
* A removed member retains plaintext and historical keys already possessed. The default interval ends at the old epoch. Future admitted confidential writes after resume use fresh material it cannot derive, assuming honest participating endpoints and suitable channels. Removal cannot claw back copied data.
* A new member receives current-and-future access by default. Historical access is a separate explicit delegation, naming an interval or snapshot; it is not implicit in joining.
* A remaining member offline through the change may fetch the committed control history and its authorized catch-up package on return. Until reconciliation, it can read cached history and queue drafts locally, but cannot emit a valid old-epoch confidential write. Padding emitted by a proxy does not heal its compromised endpoint state.

An authorized writer must authenticate the complete `(object, epoch, operation identity, ciphertext commitment, law/version)` binding. Shared AEAD keys do not establish individual writer identity against readers. Canonical quorum order cannot supply this authentication, or demonstrate that wraps exclude revoked readers. PQ authentication and PQ confidentiality need independently stated assumptions, including the consensus certificate if that certificate is claimed PQ-secure.

## 3. Two viable transition choices

### A. Atomic audience publication

**PROPOSED:** clients prepare fresh key material and exact recipient packages against an authenticated audience/device snapshot. One admitted transition simultaneously writes revocation/authority changes and the new audience epoch, wrap manifest and active marker. Confidential writes require the current active epoch; stale writers fail. The secret is generated outside Host; atomicity refers to publication of already prepared bytes, not to generating secrets inside a transaction.

This has one visible cutover and no admitted intermediate freeze. Members need not all be online if packages can be safely produced for their authenticated current device keys. It needs a source-owned combined admission/controller construction: C's ordinary invocation only reads authority, while its revocation receiver only writes authority. Reuse durable exact-image settlement, nullifiers and guards; do not claim the current APIs already expose the compound action.

The combined admission must bind recipient-device records, audience snapshot, authority preimage, history policy and epoch parent. Authority or device-key changes between preparation and publication cause contention and re-preparation. When audience privacy hides the list, ordinary public commitment equality does not establish recipient-set correctness; choose explicitly who verifies hidden membership, or whether an authorized distributor is trusted. Atomicity prevents a partial durable cutover, not malicious distributor disclosure.

Costs: preparation buffering, retry amplification under churn, a wider admission proof/codec, and transfer of whatever key protocol is chosen. Waiting for all recipient acknowledgements would add an unnecessary all-online condition unless the product explicitly demands proven receipt before cutover.

### B. Freeze, revoke, rekey, resume — preferred first specification

**PROPOSED:** first publish `Frozen(e,T)`; then revoke under current policy; then publish a fresh epoch/package manifest bound to the post-revocation audience and device generations; finally atomically publish the ready epoch and `Active(e+1)`. Separate stages can share one transition ID with distinct exact phase operations. Resume requires the specified complete manifest and authorized producer; it cannot be inferred from a local ring update.

This makes absence of secrets and offline administrators a visible, recoverable availability state. **Fresh affected confidential state writes and fresh release admissions are refused while frozen**, including writes by remaining valid members. Exact releases authorized before freeze may finish afterward; a delivered invocation still faces current receiving authority/epoch/law unless its effect was already admitted. Freeze does not retract historical disclosure. Reads of already authorized history may continue. Public metadata operations may continue only if their declared law excludes confidential effects and transition interference. An authorized old-epoch draft may be re-encrypted after resume; silently committing it under the old key defeats the boundary.

Current APIs can represent ordinary marker/wrap publications and an independent revoke, with durable retries. They do **not yet establish this complete protocol**. In particular, a freeze flag only helps if every affected invocation has a source-owned dependency on it. The inspected domain guards include authority and clock, not an arbitrary audience marker. A client-only freeze or convention that adds an optional target is insufficient. Construction must add/verify a mandatory epoch dependency and receiving guard, or an equivalent current-law enforcement path, across all affected operations. If the marker resides apart from the revoked resource, the revoke route also needs an explicit binding to it; its present resource guard does not automatically cover another cell.

Freeze should precede revoke. Revocation followed by freeze leaves a window in which an honest remaining writer can publish ciphertext readable by the removed member. Crash after freeze is safe but unavailable; another **currently authorized** manager can continue T, using new randomness if needed. No owner recovery bypass is introduced. If policy deliberately locks out every manager, stuck-frozen is a permitted administrative outcome. Abort must be an authorized new transition with explicit rights; it must not silently restore revoked grants or old keys.

Costs: more durable phase operations, an availability pause, restart ownership rules, and mandatory epoch-dependency enforcement. Its advantage is that these obligations are exposed rather than hidden inside secret preparation. Once they are proved, atomic publication can be assessed as a later reduction in phases.

## 4. What the literature answers, and what it leaves open

**READ [Grappa/GKP](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1028.pdf)**, Backendal–Balbás–Haller, EUROCRYPT 2026 full version, revised **2026-05-10**; §§1, 5.1–5.2, 6.1–6.3, 7.1. Interval access control permits compact persistent access within delegated epochs instead of retaining every transport ratchet key. Grappa has distinct admin/member CGKAs; interval progression and CGKA epochs are related but not identical. Member-channel healing plus interval updates matters; admin recovery additionally needs `RotKeys`. Its efficient construction does not provide forward security for deleted files. It explicitly excludes injected/forged control messages, proves selective rather than adaptive security, and warns GKP is not a complete file-sharing integrity/authentication model. Correctness assumes a consistent control sequence and correct delivery.

**DERIVED:** Grappa is a strong vocabulary and candidate for persistent Mini data, not a drop-in removal controller. A PQ transport substitution and active Mini authority composition need their own argument. Separate transport state erasure from persistent delegated interval state. Re-encrypting old items does not erase old copies available to former readers. Admin compromise exposes substantially more history and future-derivation material; rotating only an ordinary writer seed cannot heal it. Recovery must terminate compromised admin authority, obtain fresh uncompromised distribution material and explicitly rotate the interval state. An administrator continuing to exfiltrate cannot be cryptographically healed.

**READ [Fork-Resilient CGKA](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/394.pdf)**, Alwen–Mularczyk–Tselekounis, CRYPTO 2023, revision **2024-02-22**; §§1.1, 3.2–3.4, 6. It supports causality-respecting out-of-order processing through a history graph and an explicit description of retained transition capability. Its fork-resolution wrapper still needs a higher-level state-resolution algorithm deciding authorized membership; some members must be removed/re-added when they cannot transition. The older paper answers restart/fork questions more directly than a recent bandwidth result. It does not authorize Mini to resurrect a rejected old branch. Mini's accepted parent should choose the authoritative transition; divergent local key proposals are pending or discarded, not competing accepted audiences.

**READ [Concurrent Updates without Pruning](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1035.pdf)**, Auerbach et al., CRYPTO 2025, PDF dated **2025-06-03**; §§1.1, 4.1, 5.1–5.2. It addresses TreeKEM update-proposal cost, with random-proposer/committer assumptions for its efficient bound. Its discussion explains why encrypting to concurrently replaced keys and losing parent-hash authentication cause security trouble. MLS-Cutoff modifies update proposals; adds/removes retain MLS-style handling and a commit still establishes the root. This is useful evidence against assuming concurrent rekeys compose freely, not evidence that arbitrary concurrent Mini audience changes commute.

## 5. Distinguishing trace and remaining proof obligations

Minimal falsifier: initially Alice/Bob/Carol know epoch 7. Alice submits Bob's revoke; it commits. Alice crashes before rotation. Carol, still authorized, appends at epoch 7. Bob learns the ciphertext via any retained transport/cache and decrypts it. This falsifies **future confidentiality beginning at revoke** for the unguarded two-operation construction; it does not falsify Mini's capability revocation theorem. In choice A there is no revoke-only committed state. In choice B Carol's append fails after freeze. A second falsifier changes Carol's device generation after packages are prepared: publication must contend or use an explicitly authorized recovery policy, not activate an obsolete wrap silently.

Restart must anchor to authenticated admitted history, retain uncertain exact operation bytes, and persist cryptographic progress before emitting. Restoring a backup must not reuse AEAD nonces, sender-chain positions or spent one-time authenticators. If the client cannot establish a monotone generation anchor, provision a fresh device epoch and authorized catch-up; do not rewind the cryptographic state to match a convenient application snapshot. Losing a reply after publication must recover the published transition, not create a conflicting successor because the local ring lacks it.

Unproved: mandatory freeze/epoch enforcement across every entry point; active malicious-insider key-distribution correctness; hidden-audience verification; PQ suite/identity/certificate composition; authority/device race binding; physical client persistence and rollback fencing; interval delegation combined with Mini observations and migrations. These are bounded construction questions. None is discharged by a quorum ordering certificate or a paper title.
