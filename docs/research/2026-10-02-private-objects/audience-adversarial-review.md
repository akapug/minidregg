## Blocking counterexample: protected-object freeze does not compose through grant dependencies

`Kernel/CapabilityRevocationController.lean:207–218,229–247` requires the victim capability's policy ID equal the command target. It then loads that target's policy and calls `ObjectAudience.checkRevoke`. This fences direct revocation attached to a protected target, not every authority dependency capable of invalidating its readers.

`Theory/AuthorizationDeclaration.lean:430–461`, `capabilityAdmissibleCheck` and `Capability.admissible_iff_components`, independently require live ancestors/channels, current issuer and policy epochs, holder/scope coverage and height validity. `Kernel/ObjectAudience.lean:12–19`, `checkFresh`, checks only object identity, active mode and supplied epoch. `ResourceTransaction.lean:713` uses that check at fresh target preparation. It does not compare the activation authority image or revalidate all holders of the symmetric key.

Concrete trace: object O is active at epoch 7; Bob possesses K7 and reads using grant b descended from ancestor a whose policy is ordinary P. Carol has an independent live write grant c. An authorized manager revokes a through CapabilityRevocationController targeted at P. P has no protected audience, so checkRevoke accepts without freezing O. Bob's future authorized reads fail on the ancestor check. O remains active at epoch 7; Carol's fresh transaction is accepted with epoch 7 and valid c. Bob gets the new ciphertext through a cache, transport, remaining member, or prior replication entitlement and decrypts it with K7. No malicious encryption is needed. This violates future confidentiality beginning at semantic reader invalidation while preserving every local audience theorem inspected.

The minimal requirement is not merely “writer has current authority.” Every fresh confidential write must establish that its *encryption audience* is still permitted under the promised current entitlement contract, or that an audience invalidation fence has not changed since rekey.

## Additional invalidation paths

1. **Same-object law tightening is an actual receiving path.** `ObjectAudienceController.Valid:22–27` permits `next = old` unconditionally as an audience-preserving policy installation. `PolicyInstallReceiver.prepare:264–283` composes ordinary policy preparation with that audience check. Thus an authorized policy update can deny Bob observations while retaining active epoch 7 and its manifest. Carol can remain authorized and publish under K7. “State-preserving audience metadata” is not equivalent to preservation of read entitlement. Compare the old/new law's audience meaning, require freeze for potentially restrictive changes, or invalidate a dedicated audience generation on policy revision.
2. **Time expiration needs no mutation.** Capability admission checks `cap.notBefore ≤ height ≤ cap.notAfter` at AuthorizationDeclaration:438–440. Bob's grant expires while Carol's remains live; active epoch/key remains unchanged. A conservative authority-root comparison alone does not detect this. Either define key access independently of expiring query permission, or bind active epoch validity to the earliest relevant audience expiry/current authorization witnesses and require rekey before accepting later confidential writes.
3. **Issuer/policy/subject-key epochs and channel revocation are semantic routes.** `Theory/CredentialAuthorityEffects.lean:1104–1136` defines issuer, policy and subject-key epoch mutations; AuthorizationDeclaration:441–445 and :535–536 consume those current slots and revoked channels. I did not establish a public generic epoch/channel mutation receiver in this bounded read, so these are required compositional obligations rather than claims that every route is currently callable. Any present/future controller exposing them must participate in the same invalidation discipline. Subject-key rotation should trigger confidentiality healing only when the contract regards the old device as compromised/excluded; mere inability to sign is not erasure of its symmetric key.
4. **Scope/parent changes also matter.** Scope coverage consumes `state.parent` (AuthorizationDeclaration:436). If a controller changes the authority containment relation, reader validity can change without direct grant revocation. Include this in dependency closure; its externally exposed mutation route was not established here.

## Device binding is currently object-root binding

`PolicyInstallReceiver.AudiencePrepared:204–207` explicitly proves `deviceRoot = durable.snapshot.model.roots object`. `prepareAudience:225–239` accepts content, declaredObject or stream kind, checks its cell law, and guards that object's root. It neither resolves an authenticated roster nor interprets `State.devices` as a set of recipient-device generations. `ObjectAudienceController.Bound:37–42` compares the claimed `deviceSnapshot` only with that object root during enrollment/resume.

This is a useful object-preimage guard, but it is not a device-roster correctness claim. If the actual roster is canonically stored inside O and the distributor authenticates that schema/projection, the root can bind it. Otherwise a device record D may rotate after wrap preparation while O is unchanged; resume accepts packages for obsolete D. Minimal correction: name roster object(s), canonical roster projection/schema, authenticated recipient keys and generations, and derive their guard closure at preparation. Alternatively explicitly restrict this version to co-located authenticated roster data in O and state that restriction; do not label generic root equality device freshness. Distributor trust for wrap correctness remains an independent assumption.

## Minimal compositional remedies

**Conservative first construction:** fresh protected writes require a current audience authorization fence and clock validity. Compare against an activation image/generation covering all relevant authorization state; any potentially relevant authority/law change or expiry blocks new writes until authorized freeze/rekey/resume. Coarse refusal is safe and costly. Binding the whole authority root would block unrelated changes and ordinary policy-install effects; importantly the current resume stores the PRE-install authority root while installation writes a changed authority cell. A post-install root or dedicated generation must be computed and receipt-bound, otherwise permanent equality self-invalidates immediately.

**Preferred refined contract:** authenticated audience roster carries each recipient's authorization meaning, lineage/ancestors/channels, issuer and subject/device generation, applicable law revision, scope dependencies and validity interval. Every fresh write/release verifies an unchanged dedicated audience generation plus a source-owned proof/witness that the effective authorized audience remains valid at the commit cut. Relevant invalidation transitions advance that generation atomically or refuse until affected audiences are frozen. A reverse dependency index must itself be authenticated and complete; a client-maintained list is insufficient. Expiry must be enforced even with no state update.

**Alternative per-publication validation:** resolve authenticated recipient grants against current authority/law/clock for every confidential admission and guard all consumed projections. If one key holder is no longer permitted, refuse publication until rekey. This avoids complete reverse indexing but costs roster-scale verification and must bind hidden membership correctly. A recipient grant witness only on transport release does not protect new ciphertext already appended into accessible object storage; gate ciphertext creation/publication wherever old holders can obtain it.

Choose the contract explicitly: if key audience is deliberately independent of current query capability, expiry or key-signature rotation need not automatically shrink it, but direct/ancestor reader removal cannot then be advertised as immediate future confidentiality. Retain commit-time grandfathering of exact already committed releases; none of these remedies pretends revocation unsends bytes.

## Concrete controller recommendation over current read APIs

Recommend strong current-entitlement semantics for fresh confidential disclosures. Epoch-persistent access rights are a coherent alternative only if explicitly delegated separately: Bob may lose Host query authority while retaining epoch membership, so removal of ordinary grants does not promise future confidentiality. That changes the desired product contract and should not be introduced through implementation convenience.

Existing reusable primitives: `ResourceObservationAdmission.project/step/policyConfig/authorizeChecked` (171–230) supply canonical request/resource/context/account/clock projection and committed law admission; `AuthorizationDeclaration.capabilityAdmissibleCheck` supplies full grant predicates. However `ResourceObservationAdmission.prepare` observes a current directory root, its read candidate is unchanged, and `authorizeChecked` requires `CheckedSignature` for the requester's live query. These do not directly establish offline recipient entitlement to newly created post-state. `NativeObservationController.standing/members` (600–627) checks capability standing but not the complete current read law or device generation. Reusing `who` as the final audience proof would omit decisive conditions.

Bounded new Lean ownership:

* `Theory/AudienceRoster.lean`: canonical bounded duplicate-free entries `(subject, readCapability, deviceRegistrySource, deviceGeneration, encryptionKeyCommitment)` plus object/epoch/transition and canonical digest. Source metadata's audience/devices commitments must match the actual retained roster/projections. Enrollment records authenticate complete key-holder distribution under the honest-distributor assumption; a list of currently online intended recipients is insufficient because offline retained K holders can decrypt.
* `Kernel/RecipientReadEntitlement.lean`: source-owned internal semantic judgment for a roster entry and exact disclosed candidate view, current authority/height/clock/source law. Reuse existing capability and canonical policy machinery, but explicitly separate recipient identity evidence from a live recipient-signed query. This does not permit impersonating a query or returning plaintext to a writer as that principal. Factor the shared read-law projection rather than inventing a simpler policy evaluator. Construct a read request/root referring to the candidate disclosure, evaluate unchanged candidate state as an observation, and check required view/field projection coverage: entitlement to one narrowed field cannot authorize a whole plaintext payload. Preserve context/account dependencies where the law uses them.
* `Kernel/ConfidentialAudienceAdmission.lean`: load canonical retained roster, current source and authenticated separate device registry; check active/epoch; validate EVERY retained key holder with RecipientReadEntitlement against the exact candidate post or release view; bind resulting bytes/ciphertext digest/roster/epoch/writer identity; derive source, authority, clock, roster and device read guards. Expose a Prepared/Accepted constructor unavailable to callers forging a roster. Compose it into `ResourceTransaction.PreparedTarget` for ciphertext writes and a dedicated confidential release controller for export. Current public selective release rejects protected sources by absent epoch, which is safe; do not weaken that check to make it the confidential route.

Native client preparation must authenticate the complete roster and device packages, perform the current-entitlement check before honest encryption, and retain exact packages/command across uncertain replies. This is a useful initial client realization but insufficient as an adversarial server admission boundary: preparation can become stale before append, custom clients can omit it, and encrypted post-bytes can differ. Final admission rechecks all recipients at the commit cut over exact candidate bytes/view and guards the inputs. Under an honest writer, payload/key distribution and fresh key generation remain explicit assumptions; the server does not prove encryption correctness merely by checking digest/manifests. If the read law depends on plaintext hidden from Host, this version needs a trusted executor, supported disclosure-policy restriction, or a proof-backed confidential evaluator; checking ciphertext instead is not the same law.

Per-audience dependency invalidation is a later optimization of the full all-holder check, not its replacement specification. Cache successful judgments under authenticated dependency projections and validity bounds; prove unchanged projections imply unchanged entitlement. Clock/height advancement still invalidates expiration/time-sensitive laws, and relevant revocation/law/device changes invalidate the cache. Ordinary global authority and clock guards initially close the preparation-to-commit race; they do not establish entitlement by themselves and authority-root equality does not solve expiry.
