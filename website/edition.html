What this edition carries

The next build of the Mini demo, sorted three ways: what is built, what is still being built, and what is not in this edition. Nothing here is running for the public yet.

Where things stand. The demo box still serves an earlier rehearsal with every login closed, and the join page is closed until the owner opens it. The door opens on the owner's word, and the demo shuts down on 11 November 2026, when everything on it is erased. Read the overview for what that means for anything you put there.

Built, for when the door opens

These are on the repository's main branch and in the build that will ship. Each is listed with its evidence on the status page.

  • One admission path. Documents, grants, money, programs and applications all go through the same admission. A refusal names the clause that refused it, and the web page shows the one request that would have been admitted.
  • A lost reply is not a lost write. The client keeps the exact call before it sends it. If the answer never arrives, the attempt stays undecided and you retry the same bytes, so nothing happens twice.
  • Join in the browser. A name, a hosted key and one link to your session. The free route has a fixed number of seats. The paid route costs 50 DREGG a week on Solana mainnet.
  • Web pages for every command. Every shell command has a form on the web, derived from the command grammar (203 commands in all), and the outcome of each is announced to screen readers.
  • Export my world. One archive of your documents, receipts and key, streamed to you and then deleted from the box. One export per member every ten minutes.
  • Research consent. An unchecked box at the door. Your choice is recorded as a fact in your own world, and you can see and change it on the export page.
  • Stories and laws. A built-in tale under its sealed law, and the means to install a law on your own document and watch it judge your next write.
  • Objective Bend. The bend command is in this edition.
  • Discord. The same subject and receipts over the Discord gateway as on the web.
  • Agent residents. An agent can live in a room as a member on a shared model key. Each member gets a share of the spend ceiling: on the demo's numbers, $1 a day and $4 in all.
  • A subscriber tier. Pay for a week and an invitation arrives in your inbox to a room with a house agent on the glm-5.3-flash model.
  • An app market. Any Sandstorm package from the real store, 13 featured apps listed first, each with an interface an agent can drive beneath the HTML. File, calendar and git apps work through WebDAV, CalDAV and git, with request bodies up to 8 MiB.
  • Abuse limits. Limits on connections and in-flight requests per member at the web gateway, and size classes (memory, CPU, tasks) for applications.

Still being built

These may or may not land before the door opens. A row stays "in progress" on the status page until it is on main.

  • The platform cell. The cell kind and its install receiver are built; the genesis seed, replay, Host operations and births priced from the cell are not.
  • A fast Store open. Compaction is built. Opening a 483-record Store took 160 to 175 seconds before it. The next step, a checkpoint that carries the world so an open replays only the records after it, is stated and being proved.
  • Invite codes. A reserve of free seats that need an invite code is on main but not yet on the box.
  • More qualified apps. Each app earns its place with one write beneath the HTML; the market is growing toward two to three dozen. Two browsers co-editing one app is not working yet.
  • Effect Plans. Programs that transfer from their purse and commit writes. The first keystone is proved; the rest is being finished.
  • A per-member request rate in the Host. Stated and proved, not yet wired in.

Not in this edition

  • The fn transport, and a second node.
  • The oracle as an application.
  • A networked private-computation service.
  • Strangers at the join page, until the owner opens the door.
